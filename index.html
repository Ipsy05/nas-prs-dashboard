The 5 findings that go beyond "COVID caused learning loss"
PARADOX

Recovery is unequal — not just incomplete

Average score below 2017 + proficiency above 2017 = top half recovered, bottom half fell further. Reporting national averages alone hides this growing inequality.

COUNTERINTUITIVE

Punjab went from #28 to #1 in 7 years

Scored 58.7% in 2017 — below national average, ranked near the bottom. Now the top state nationally. The SCERT-DIET teacher data review model is the most replicable success story in this dataset.

STRUCTURAL

Maths is classroom-dependent in a way Language is not

Language dropped 4.7pp in COVID. Maths dropped 7.6pp — 61% harder. Children can practice language at home. Maths requires structured procedural practice with a teacher. Any future disruption will hit Maths first.

REVERSAL

Private school advantage — busted at Grade 3

Private schools led in 2017 (70%) and 2021 (67%). In 2024 they fell to 64% while state govt schools rose to 65%. NIPUN Bharat\'s structured pedagogy outperformed private schools\' resource advantage.

DATA QUALITY

The 2017 vs 2024 drop for top states is partly a measurement artefact

Karnataka scored 75.7% in 2017 but only 65% in 2024 — an apparent 10.7pp fall. However, 2017 measured Govt+Aided schools only (which score lower). 2024 added private schools. In states like Karnataka where private schools may score lower than govt schools, this inflates the apparent decline. The real gap is smaller — but how much smaller remains unquantifiable without school-type-specific state data.

6 recommendations — each grounded in specific data
01

Target the bottom 35% in Maths separately

Math proficiency is 65% but average is 60%. The top half recovered. Design a targeted remedial Maths programme for below-proficiency children — not just extend NIPUN. Evidence: IRT paradox.

02

Study and replicate the Punjab model

58.7% in 2017 → 70% in 2024. An 11pp gain. SCERT-DIET partnership + teacher-led data reviews. 5–6 lagging states should be supported to adapt this. Evidence: State data.

03

Switch from average-score to percentile reporting

A state can improve its average while its bottom quintile falls further. PARAKH should publish decile distributions. Tie NIPUN funding to bottom-30% improvement, not overall averages.

04

Make SC/ST gap closure a funding condition

8pp below General in 2024. Gap widened from 6pp in 2017. Standard FLN interventions aren\'t reaching ST children. Tie state NIPUN funding release to measurable equity outcomes. Evidence: Group chart.

05

Extend structured pedagogy to private schools

Private schools have no NIPUN mandate and fell 3pp in 2024. At Grade 3, structured pedagogy beats unstructured teaching. Extend NCERT competency frameworks as a condition for private school recognition renewal.

06

Design Maths for disruption-resilience

Maths fell 61% harder than Language in COVID because it cannot be practiced at home. Design structured take-home Maths worksheets and parent guidance cards into NIPUN — ready for the next disruption.

WHAT IS THIS SURVEY?

NAS / PRS — India\'s National Learning Assessment

The National Achievement Survey (NAS) and its 2024 successor PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan (PRS) are India\'s largest system-level assessments of student learning. No individual child receives a score or rank. The survey measures the health of the education system itself — providing district, state, and national evidence for policymakers.

NAS 2017 · 13 Nov 2017
  • 22 lakh students, 701 districts
  • Govt + Aided schools only
  • Grade 3: Language + Math
  • First LO-based NAS
NAS 2021 · 12 Nov 2021
  • 34 lakh students, 720 districts
  • Govt + Aided + Private
  • Grade 3: Language + Math + EVS

PRS 2024 · 4 Dec 2024
  • 21.15 lakh students, 781 districts
  • Govt + Aided + Private
  • Grade 3: Language + Math (NEP aligned)
  • PARAKH-administered
Critical: Grade 3 is the ONLY grade directly comparable across all 3 rounds. NAS 2017 excluded private schools; 2021 and 2024 include them. This makes direct comparison approximate — the true COVID drop in like-for-like terms is likely larger than 6.7pp.
What this % means: these are NOT performance scores. They show each state's share of the national school sample that round — i.e. sampling weight, driven mainly by how many schools a state has. A state's % can shift between rounds even if nothing changed about its schools, simply because PARAKH changed how many schools it sampled nationally (42,166 in 2017 → 28,089 in 2021 → 27,741 in 2024).

A real "was any state over/under-sampled?" answer needs two things this dataset doesn't have: (1) each state's total school count, ideally split by Govt / Aided / Private, to check if sample share tracks school share; and (2) district-level sampling counts, to check if the sample was spread evenly or concentrated in a few districts within a state. Key findings from the data
EVS fell hardest (−6pp avg). Language practiced at home; EVS and Maths require school structure. COVID\'s effect is directly visible in subject-level differences.
Govt Aided was BEST in 2017. Language 68.2%, Maths 64.9%, EVS 67.4% — highest of ALL school types. Yet fell heavily in 2021.
Central Govt scored LOWEST in 2021. Language 56%, Maths 52%. Expected to be highest. Many Central Govt schools are residential — COVID completely disrupted boarding arrangements.
Girls led in BOTH Language and Maths in 2017. Language: 67.4 vs 65.9 (boys). Maths: 63.2 vs 62.7. By 2021 both genders equal — girls' advantage erased by COVID.
OBC fell most in Language (−5.3pp). OBC: 67.3→62.0. SC fell least (−3.6pp): 65.6→62.0. SC was already lower — less room to fall. Different groups had different COVID buffers.
Rural slightly led Urban in Language 2017 (66.8 vs 66.2). In EVS, Urban led (64.4 vs 63.6). Rural students read in home language; urban EVS knowledge is broader.
